## Governing question

The project is not primarily testing coding benchmark improvement. The target is verified, reality-constrained plasticity of an explicit capability/reasoning algebra `A_t`: operators + lawful compositions + verified laws/scopes + provenance.

Always test closure before invention. A target already in `Closure(A_t)` is a scientifically valuable negative for primitive formation. The major positive gate is stronger than reuse: does an acquired operator change which later operator is discoverable under the same frozen discovery process?

## V32 — external law miner

Scanned the fixed QuixBugs corpus for composition laws rather than assuming V30b's local equivalence was universal.

Key result: no tested pair (`CMP|BIN`, `CMP|CONST`, `BIN|CONST`) commuted across all supporting functions; no unary operator was globally idempotent or involutive across the support.

## Current strongest interpretation

The explicit developmental object should be treated roughly as

`A_t = (O_t, L_t, V, D_t)`

where `O_t` are verified generators/capabilities, `L_t` are scoped verified composition/equivalence laws, `V` is the external authority boundary, and `D_t` is the frozen discovery procedure induced by the current algebra.

Two distinct expansion mechanisms now have bounded evidence:

1. **Interface/frontier expansion**: a prior verified capability exposes a new representation/interface where a later capability becomes discoverable (V24b-V27).
2. **Description-length expansion**: verified chunking lowers effective program cost so a successor becomes searchable under the same fixed horizon (V28b-V30b).

The next decisive gate must combine these with a genuinely closure-expanding external operator, rather than only a predeclared meta-language symbol or shorthand chunk.
