# Developmental Reachability Preorder

Date: 2026-08-15 NZST

Status: mathematical synthesis of the bounded quotient/closure evidence. Definitions and elementary consequences below are formal; empirical relevance is separately bounded by the cited experiment families.

## 1. Setup

Let:

- `X` be a universe of executable behaviours/transformations;
- `A` be the currently admitted capability basis;
- `Cl(A)` be the lawful closure generated by `A` under the currently admitted composition rules;
- `V` be verifier authority, inducing observational equivalence `~_V` on `X`;
- `B` be an optional finite composition/search/resource horizon.

Write `[x]_V` for the verifier-equivalence class of behaviour `x`.

The system should not identify a candidate capability with its literal syntax. The relevant object is what becomes *verifiably reachable* after admitting it.

## 2. Verifier-indexed developmental reachability

For candidate `o`, define its verifier-indexed reachable quotient set:

`R(A,V;o) = { [x]_V : x in Cl(A union {o}) }`.

This forgets literal implementation details that do not alter verifier-visible lawful reachability while retaining every verifier-distinguishable consequence of admission.

### Developmental identity

Define:

`o ==_(A,V)^dev p`

iff

`R(A,V;o) = R(A,V;p)`.

This is an equivalence relation because equality of sets is reflexive, symmetric and transitive.

When `V` is extensionally complete for the finite world, this reduces to equality of exact generated closures:

`Cl(A union {o}) = Cl(A union {p})`.

This is the exact identity used in the finite closure experiments.

## 3. Developmental preorder

Define on literal candidates:

`o <=_(A,V)^dev p`

iff

`R(A,V;o) subseteq R(A,V;p)`.

This relation is a **preorder**:

1. reflexive: every set is a subset of itself;
2. transitive: subset inclusion is transitive.

It need not be antisymmetric on literal candidates because distinct literals can induce the same reachable quotient set.

After quotienting by developmental identity, define:

`[o]^dev <= [p]^dev`

iff

`R(A,V;o) subseteq R(A,V;p)`.

This is a **partial order** on developmental equivalence classes.

Therefore the mathematically safe generic object is a verifier-indexed developmental reachability **preorder**, or a **poset after quotienting by equal reachability**. Calling it a lattice requires additional join/meet existence and uniqueness that need not hold.

## 4. Capability gain

Define the verified gain from admitting `o`:

`Gain(A,V;o) = R(A,V;o) \ R(A,V;empty)`

where `R(A,V;empty)` is the quotient reachability of the old basis alone.

A literal change with empty gain is not capability extension under this definition, even if its syntax is new.

A candidate `o` is an **EXTEND** event when:

`Gain(A,V;o) != empty`.

Two syntactically different operators with identical gain and identical resulting reachability are the same developmental capability at `(A,V)`.

## 5. Resource/composition horizon

Let `Cl_B(A)` be lawful closure reachable within horizon/budget `B`, with monotone budgets:

`B0 <= B1 => Cl_B0(A) subseteq Cl_B1(A)`.

Define bounded novelty of candidate `q` relative to state `A`:

`Novel(q | A,V,B)` iff `[q]_V` is not represented in `{[x]_V : x in Cl_B(A)}`.

Then budget monotonicity gives the one-way law:

`not Novel(q | A,V,B0) => not Novel(q | A,V,B1)` for `B1 >= B0`.

Equivalently, increasing lawful composition/search budget may make apparent novelty **collapse**, but cannot make a behaviour that was already reachable at a smaller budget become unreachable solely by enlarging the budget.

The converse is false:

`Novel(q | A,V,B0)` does **not** imply `Novel(q | A,V,B1)`.

V114 supplies an exact finite witness: two Z/4Z operator orbits are distinct at one candidate occurrence but mutually reachable at two occurrences and generate the same exact closure.

## 6. Verifier authority moves in the opposite structural direction

If `V0 subseteq V1`, then `~_(V1)` refines `~_(V0)`.

Thus increasing verifier authority may split previously merged observable behaviours. In the quotient partition this is a **REFINE** event.

This is structurally different from increasing `B`:

- `V up` can split observational classes;
- `B up` can collapse apparent novelty by exposing lawful constructions.

So novelty should be indexed by at least `(A,V,B)`, not treated as an absolute property of a literal operator.

## 7. Four state transitions

The current framework distinguishes four non-equivalent changes.

### EXTEND

Admission enlarges verifier-visible lawful reachability:

`R(A,V;o)` strictly contains `R(A,V;empty)`.

### REFINE

Verifier authority increases and splits an old observational class without necessarily changing executable closure.

### COLLAPSE

A stronger lawful composition/resource horizon or enlarged old basis reveals that an apparently distinct candidate is already reachable/developmentally equivalent.

### RETRACT

Governance removes or narrows a previously admitted capability because later verifier evidence falsifies its admissible scope or protected behaviour.

RETRACT is not the inverse of REFINE: it is a governance transition on admitted state, not merely a change in observational granularity.

## 8. Coordinate orbits are only a sufficient intermediate quotient

Let `G_A` be invertible coordinate transformations already realizable by `A`. The orbit

`Orb_A(o) = { g(o) : g in G_A }`

can provide a useful syntax/presentation quotient.

But orbit identity need not equal developmental identity.

The safe relation is:

`same old-coordinate orbit  =>  candidate for same developmental identity`

subject to lawful verifier-preserving action, not the converse.

Fresh finite experiments show the converse can fail: separate coordinate orbits can generate the same exact closure. Therefore developmental closure identity is strictly more semantic than literal-coordinate orbit identity.

## 9. Compression / description-cost consequence

If several literal repairs belong to one developmental class, retaining one governed class representation can reduce repeated search/description cost. But compression is an empirical/resource claim, not implied by quotient identity alone.

A proper compression test must charge:

- class/operator representation;
- invocation/scope rule;
- verifier/provenance requirements;
- reconstruction/search cost;
- exceptions/withdrawal conditions.

This keeps `compression` distinct from mere displacement of complexity.

## 10. Ratchet interpretation

A Reliability-Ratchet-style admission object is therefore better written as something like:

`C = (D, S, E, W)`

where:

- `D` is developmental reachability identity/class;
- `S` is admissible invocation scope;
- `E` is verifier/provenance evidence;
- `W` is withdrawal/revision condition.

The retained object is not merely a patch token. It is a reversible, evidence-indexed hypothesis about a verified change in reachable behaviour.

## 11. Evidence alignment

The current empirical sequence supports different pieces at different strength:

- V101P: natural-code example where widening lawful composition depth removes one apparent missing-operator claim;
- V104: coordinate/presentation quotient invariance and verifier refinement in exact finite B2/GF(3) worlds;
- V105: distinct quotient classes with directed reachability consequences in fresh GF(4);
- V106B–V109: quotient-level repair transport and verifier-induced relation selection on external QuixBugs programs;
- V110: blind recurrence of quotient-level repair structure in actual historical QuixBugs bugs;
- V112B: exact negative null result (`p = 0.091375911...`), showing retrospective recurrence alone is insufficient;
- V113: coordinate orbit identity is finer than exact generated-closure identity in GF(5);
- V114: composition-horizon sensitivity in fresh Z/4Z;
- scope/revocation line: retained operation and admissible invocation conditions can separate, with later evidence causing narrowing or revocation.

These experiments do not establish a universal theorem that real-world reasoning systems possess a small or tractable developmental poset.

## 12. Current strongest bounded formulation

> A candidate capability should be identified operationally by the verifier-distinguishable lawful behaviours its admission makes reachable, relative to the current capability basis and resource horizon. Literal representations that induce the same reachable quotient set are developmentally equivalent; their equivalence classes form a reachability poset under inclusion. Stronger verifier authority may refine observational identity, while deeper lawful composition may collapse apparent novelty.

## Claim boundary

The formal preorder/quotient facts follow from the definitions. What remains empirical is whether these objects are useful, discoverable, compressive and predictive in broad natural reasoning/code domains, and whether a system can autonomously construct the relevant representations, scopes and separators without the researcher supplying the decisive meta-language.
