## Symmetry quotient

Carrier names are presentation. The symmetric group (S_3) acts on binary functions by simultaneous conjugation of both inputs and the output:

[
(pcdot f)(p(a),p(b)) = p(f(a,b)).
]

It acts on capability sets pointwise.

The experiment computes the orbit quotient of the 827 behavioral states under this action. This is an **isomorphism quotient of behavioral states**, not equality under fixed function labels.

The only extra symmetry applied here is carrier relabeling.

## Four adversarial fixtures

### AB-like recombination

Two states have the same present protected observation. A lawful recombination continuation yields different protected consequences. They must not be quotiented by present observation alone.

### Which-path fabrication

Two latent states are not distinguished by the protected path predicate. A probe maps them to two visibly distinct states. Because the probe is not nonfabricating for that predicate, the post-probe distinction cannot be attributed backwards.

### Measurement / observer boundary

Two states are contextually equivalent under the protected observation language. An external selector attempts to choose different transition regimes for them. The selector is rejected because it does not descend through consequential equivalence.

### Bell-like factorisation

Two joint relation states have identical componentwise local-support summaries but different protected joint consequences. Any observation semantics claimed to factor through those local summaries is rejected.

This is a structural factorisation counterexample, not a claim to reproduce Bell's theorem or quantum probabilities.
