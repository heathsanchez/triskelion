# CROSS-WORLD NUCLEUS INVARIANCE V1 — prospective precommit

Status: **FROZEN BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION OR OUTCOME EXECUTION**

## Question

Does the same developmental nucleus

[
N = {	extbf{Differentiate},	extbf{Mediate},	extbf{Integrate}}
]

regenerate recursively useful verified structure across source-distinct finite worlds whose carriers, alphabets, surface encodings, and causal organization differ?

This experiment is a representation-invariance test, not a search for another domain-specific stack.

## Frozen nucleus

The internal developmental roles are exactly the same in every world.

### D — Differentiate

Given an exact residual target and the currently active feature set, find a minimum sufficient parent set under a generic finite-function criterion:

- target is a deterministic function of the candidate parents on the complete carrier;
- each selected parent is essential on the observed support;
- search is in increasing parent-set cardinality with stable world-independent ordering.

### M — Mediate

Compile the exact finite relation induced between the selected parents and the externally verified target.

No world-specific operator name, algebraic law, graph primitive, temporal primitive, Boolean connective, string operator, or arithmetic formula is exposed to the nucleus.

### I — Integrate

Install the verified mediator as a persistent executable child with:

- stable identity;
- exact finite semantics;
- parent dependencies;
- authority/provenance;
- revocation lineage.

The child becomes part of the feature language available to the next episode.

## External authority

Authority is not counted as an internal nuclear primitive.

Every world supplies an exact verifier over its complete finite carrier.

OPEN evidence is permitted as a control but may not promote structure.

Nothing self-certifies.

## Recursion criterion

Recursion is not supplied as a fourth operation.

It is present only when:

[
C_t in mathrm{Parents}(C_{t+1})
]

and targeted removal of (C_t) removes later reach while exact restoration restores it.

## Cross-world adapters

The same nucleus implementation must run unchanged over all frozen adapters below.

### W1 — Binary Cartesian world

- carrier: full (2^5) Cartesian product;
- five opaque binary primitive coordinates;
- output alphabet: binary;
- surface values: integers 0/1.

### W2 — Ternary Cartesian world

- carrier: full (3^4) Cartesian product;
- four opaque ternary primitive coordinates;
- output alphabet: ternary;
- surface values: integers 0/1/2.

### W3 — Symbolic string world

- carrier: every length-4 string over three opaque symbols;
- four primitive character-position observations;
- output alphabet: three distinct opaque symbols;
- nucleus receives symbolic tokens, not numeric encodings.

### W4 — Temporal history world

- carrier: every length-2 history over two opaque binary channels;
- primitives are the four opaque observations available on the history carrier;
- the adapter exposes them only as feature vectors; it does not label any primitive as "past", "present", "memory", or "state";
- output alphabet: binary.

### W5 — Graph-observation world

- carrier: deterministic finite directed graph nodes generated before evaluation;
- primitive features are opaque local graph observations derived by the adapter from node identity and adjacency;
- the nucleus receives only categorical feature vectors, not edges, graph terminology, or graph traversal operations;
- output alphabet: ternary.

The graph adapter must pass a pre-evaluation diversity check: at least four distinct primitive feature vectors and at least three primitive features with nonconstant support.

## Hidden relation generation

Each world generates a six-generation child chain mechanically from the frozen world seed.

For generation (t):

[
C_t = R_t(P_t, Q_t)
]

where:

- (P_1) is a primitive feature;
- for (t>1), (P_t=C_{t-1});
- (Q_t) is a mechanically scheduled primitive feature;
- (R_t) is a deterministic hash-generated finite relation over the world alphabet.

The world generator may reject and advance to the next hash-generated relation only when a frozen structural admissibility condition fails.

Required admissibility:

1. both declared parents are essential on the complete carrier;
2. target is novel relative to all active parent features at generation start;
3. for (t>1), no pair of original primitive features alone is sufficient for the target;
4. the immediately previous child occurs in at least one minimum sufficient parent set;
5. target alphabet remains within the frozen world alphabet.

No outcome-dependent protocol change is permitted.

## Candidate nuclei

Every world executes the same arms:

- NONE
- D
- M
- I
- DM
- DI
- MI
- DMI
- DMI_COLD
- ORACLE_STACK

### DMI_COLD

Full DMI operators are available, but integrated developmental state is deleted before each closed episode.

### ORACLE_STACK

Receives the declared true parent identities from the hidden world generator and may compile/install their verified relation.

It is an engineering upper bound, not a nucleus candidate.

## D-absent control

If M is present but D is absent, M may search parent subsets blindly in the same increasing cardinality space and compile a candidate finite relation for each subset.

Each attempted parent subset is charged as one **verifier candidate check**.

A fixed blind-search budget of 12 parent subsets per episode is frozen across every world.

This permits D to be shown reducible if blind mediation reaches the same regenerative depth under the resource bound.

## M-absent control

No novel finite relation may be compiled.

Reuse of already active exact features remains allowed.

## I-absent control

A verified mediator may solve the current episode transiently but is not available as a parent in later episodes.

## World-independent one-child boundary

At most one new mediator may be integrated per closed episode.

No arm may reconstruct a missing historical chain recursively inside one target episode.

This ensures later reach is caused by retained developmental capital.

## OPEN authority control

Before G1 each world exposes an incomplete positive-evidence packet.

Every arm must return `UNKNOWN` with zero state mutation.

## Causal ablation

After DMI completes G4 in each world:

1. remove exactly G3 and every installed descendant depending on G3;
2. re-run the frozen G4 target;
3. G4 must become unreachable under the identical one-child/resource protocol;
4. restore exactly the sealed G3 child;
5. G4 must verify again.

## Metamorphosis / counterevidence

After DMI completes G6 in each world, external authority changes the G3 consequence to a distinct mechanically selected admissible relation.

The system must:

1. revoke G3;
2. revoke every descendant depending on G3;
3. retain unaffected G1/G2;
4. construct and verify G3';
5. regenerate G4' through G6' using the same D/M/I implementation.

## Representation metamorphism

For W1–W4, a second run applies a frozen bijective relabelling to every primitive value and output token visible to the nucleus while preserving the hidden extensional world up to isomorphism.

Required invariants:

- verdict class unchanged;
- DMI recursive depth unchanged;
- selected parent-set cardinalities unchanged;
- causal ablation status unchanged;
- metamorphosis status unchanged.

Exact feature IDs need not match across relabellings.

## Headline gates

C1. Same nucleus source code and role definitions are used in every world.

C2. OPEN evidence yields UNKNOWN and zero mutation for every world/arm.

C3. DMI reaches G6 in all five headline worlds.

C4. G2–G6 use the previous verified child as a parent in all five worlds.

C5. DMI passes G3 causal remove/restore in all five worlds.

C6. DMI passes G3'→G6' metamorphosis in all five worlds.

C7. DMI_COLD fails to reach G6 in all five worlds.

C8. Removing I reduces recursive reach in every headline world.

C9. Removing M reduces recursive reach in every headline world.

C10. Removing D either reduces recursive reach or increases verifier-candidate work in every headline world.

C11. Representation metamorphism preserves the frozen invariants for W1–W4.

C12. No world-specific operator name or target formula occurs in nucleus role code.

## Sweep criteria

Strong cross-world invariance requires:

- DMI G6 reach in at least 120/125 sweep worlds;
- DMI post-counterevidence G6' regeneration in at least 115/125;
- DMI causal ablation pass in at least 120/125;
- DMI_COLD G6 reach in at most 10/125;
- DM and DI each lose regenerative reach relative to DMI in at least 115/125;
- MI either loses reach or uses strictly more verifier-candidate checks than DMI in at least 115/125.

The experiment must separately report any world in which a proper subset matches DMI.

## Claim boundary

A full PASS would establish only bounded finite cross-world invariance of the D/M/I developmental pattern over the five frozen adapters and resource protocol.

It would not establish:

- metaphysical fundamentality of three;
- unrestricted open-world intelligence;
- natural-language or natural-code sufficiency;
- substrate-free invention;
- that D/M/I is uniquely minimal;
- that every physical, biological, or cognitive system implements this triad.

The intended claim is narrower:

> the same small developmental law can regenerate recursively useful verified structure across materially different finite representations without redesigning the nucleus for each world.
